## What Is a Project Progress Bar for Notion?

A project progress bar for Notion is a visual indicator embedded directly in a Notion page that shows how far along a project, goal, or task list is. Unlike Notion's built-in formula-based progress hacks, an embedded widget updates visually in real time and requires no database setup, letting you see completion at a glance without any spreadsheet logic.

Most Notion users resort to one of three approaches: emoji bars copied from templates, rollup formulas tied to a task database, or external apps they check separately. Each has a real cost: emoji bars are static, formula setups break when project structure changes, and separate apps mean context-switching out of your workspace. An embeddable progress bar solves all three problems at once.

## How to Add a Progress Bar to Notion?

To add a progress bar to Notion using Blocs, open the widget at [blocs.me/progress-bar-widget](https://blocs.me/progress-bar-widget), copy the embed URL, then in Notion type `/embed`, paste the URL, and press Enter. The progress bar appears inline on your page — no account or signup needed for the free version.

### Step-by-step setup

1. Go to [blocs.me/progress-bar-widget](https://blocs.me/progress-bar-widget) and click **Get the widget**.
2. Copy the embed URL shown on the page.
3. Open your Notion page and type `/embed` in an empty block.
4. Paste the URL and press Enter. Notion loads the widget inline.
5. Resize the embed block to fit your layout. Done.

With a **Blocs Pro** account, you can open widget settings to set a custom start value, end value, label, and color theme before copying the URL — so each project page can have its own uniquely configured bar.

## Why Not Just Use Notion Formulas for a Progress Bar?

Notion formula-based progress bars require a linked database, a rollup column, and a text formula using repeated characters. They work but break when you restructure your database, don't render as true visual bars, and can't be reused across different pages without duplicating the database setup. An embedded widget is portable, visual, and requires zero formula knowledge.

The formula approach also forces you to represent progress as text like `████░░░░` rather than an actual styled bar, which is harder to read at a glance and impossible to customize without rewriting the formula. For anyone who wants a clean, real visual, an embed is the simpler answer. You can read more about the options in the guide on [how to track progress in Notion](https://blocs.me/blog/how-to-track-progress-in-notion).

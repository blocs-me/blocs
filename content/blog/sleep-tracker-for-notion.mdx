## Building a Sleep Log Database in Notion

If you want to record actual sleep times and durations, a Notion database is the right foundation. Here's a simple structure that works well alongside an embedded widget:

### Recommended database properties

- **Date** (Date property) — the night you're logging
- **Bedtime** (Text or Time) — when you got into bed
- **Wake time** (Text or Time) — when you woke up
- **Sleep duration** (Number, in hours) — calculated manually or via formula
- **Sleep quality** (Select: Poor / OK / Good / Great)
- **Notes** (Text) — caffeine, stress, exercise, anything relevant

You can add a Notion formula property to calculate duration automatically from bedtime and wake time, though Notion's formula support for time math is limited. Most people just enter the number directly.

Pair this database with an embedded habit tracker widget on the same page. The widget gives you the visual streak and quick daily check-in; the database gives you the raw data for patterns over time.

## Why Embed Instead of Using a Separate App?

Most sleep tracking apps (phone-based or wearable-connected) require you to leave your workspace entirely. You track sleep in one app, tasks in another, habits somewhere else. Context switching kills momentum.

The Notion-first approach keeps everything together. Your sleep log, habit streaks, and daily plan are all on the same page. You check off last night's sleep habit the same moment you review today's tasks. That friction reduction is meaningful — [sleep researchers note](https://www.sleepfoundation.org/sleep-hygiene/sleep-tracking) that consistent logging, even simple yes/no habit tracking, correlates with better sleep outcomes over time.

Embedding a widget instead of building a complex Notion database setup also matters. Manual database setups require discipline to maintain. A widget that's already there, on your dashboard, with a visible streak is a much stronger behavioral nudge.

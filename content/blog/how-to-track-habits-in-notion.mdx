## Why Not Just Use a Notion Database for Habits?

Notion databases are genuinely flexible, and if you enjoy building systems, a habit-tracking database is satisfying to set up. But there are real friction points:

- **Formula complexity:** Calculating streaks in Notion requires multi-step formulas that break when you miss a day or change the structure. Most people on Reddit's r/Notion community report spending more time maintaining the template than actually using it.
- **No visual feedback:** A database row of checkboxes doesn't communicate momentum the way a streak counter or completion chart does. Behavioral research consistently shows that visual progress indicators increase follow-through on habit goals.
- **Template drift:** Free Notion habit templates from sites like [Notion's official template gallery](https://www.notion.com/templates) are a good starting point, but they require regular maintenance and don't automatically generate analytics.

An embedded widget removes the maintenance burden entirely. It's always up to date, handles its own data logic, and gives you the feedback loop that makes habits actually stick.

## Habit Tracking Best Practices (That Work in Any System)

The tool matters less than the setup. Whether you use Blocs or a Notion database, these principles apply:

- **Keep the list short:** Research from behavioral scientists like BJ Fogg (author of *Tiny Habits*) suggests starting with 2–3 habits, not 10. Complexity kills compliance.
- **Make it visible:** Your tracker should be on a page you open every day — your Notion dashboard or daily note, not buried three levels deep.
- **Pair it with an anchor:** Attach your habit check-in to something you already do (morning coffee, end-of-day review) to reduce the cognitive load of remembering.
- **Review weekly, not just daily:** Weekly analytics (available in Blocs Pro) show patterns that daily check-ins hide. A habit you think you're doing well on might reveal a consistent Wednesday skip.

## What Does Notion Give You Natively for Goal Tracking?

Notion's database system is genuinely capable for goal tracking out of the box. You can create a "Goals" database with properties for category, target date, a numeric progress field, and a status select. Formula properties let you calculate a completion percentage automatically. Views like Board (Kanban) or Gallery let you see goals by status at a glance, and the Timeline view maps goals against dates.

Where native Notion falls short is **real-time visual feedback**. There's no built-in widget that shows a live progress bar, a daily habit streak, or a countdown to a deadline. For that, you need to embed something external.

## How to Set Up a Goals Database in Notion

### Step 1: Create the Database

In any Notion page, type `/database` and choose "Table — Full page." Name it "Goals 2026" or similar. Add the following properties:

- **Goal** (Title) — the goal name, e.g., "Run a 5K"
- **Category** (Select) — Health, Career, Finance, Learning, etc.
- **Target Date** (Date)
- **Progress %** (Number, formatted as Percent)
- **Status** (Select: Not Started, In Progress, Done)
- **Notes** (Text) — weekly reflection notes

### Step 2: Add Filtered Views

Add a Board view grouped by "Status" so you can drag goals from "Not Started" to "In Progress" to "Done." Add a Gallery view filtered to show only "In Progress" goals — this becomes your active dashboard. Hide done goals from your main view to reduce noise.

### Step 3: Link Milestones as Sub-Pages

Each goal row can open into a full Notion page. Use that sub-page for milestones, action steps, and weekly check-ins. This keeps your goals database clean at the top level while giving you space for detailed planning inside each goal.

## Why Should You Embed Widgets for Goal Tracking in Notion?

A database tells you *what* you want to achieve; a habit tracker tells you *whether you showed up today*; a progress bar tells you *how far you've come*. Having all three in one Notion page means you check your goals, log your actions, and see your progress without opening a second app — which is the most common reason goal systems collapse.

## What Does a Complete Goal Tracking Setup Look Like?

Here's a practical layout for a single goal page in Notion, using "Get fit in 2026" as the example:

- **Top of page:** Progress Bar widget set to 0-100% (weight lost, workouts completed, etc.)

- **Sidebar or callout block:** Countdown Timer to your target date (e.g., 90 days out)

This setup takes about 15 minutes to build. Everything updates live. You open one Notion page and know exactly where you stand.

## FAQs

### Does Notion have built-in goal tracking?

Notion doesn't have a dedicated goal-tracking feature, but its database system is flexible enough to build one. You can create a goals database with progress percentages, status properties, and target dates. For visual progress feedback — live bars, habit streaks, countdowns — you'll need to embed external widgets like those from Blocs.

### What is the best free widget for goal tracking in Notion?

The Blocs Habit Tracker is the best free option for most people. It lets you define daily habits tied to your goals and check them off directly inside Notion. No sign-up needed. The Pomodoro Timer is a close second for goal-focused work sessions. Both are free and embed with a single URL paste.

### Can I track multiple goals in one Notion workspace?

Yes. Build a single "Goals 2026" database and use filtered views to see goals by category or status. Each goal row opens into its own sub-page where you can embed individual widgets. You can have a fitness goal page with a progress bar, a reading goal page with a countdown, and a savings goal page with a habit tracker — all inside the same workspace.

### Is Blocs a subscription?

No. Blocs Pro is a one-time payment of $17 with lifetime access. There's no monthly or annual fee. The free tier (Habit Tracker, Pomodoro Timer, Water Tracker) is free forever with no account required.

### What's the difference between tracking habits and tracking goals in Notion?

Goals are outcomes you want to reach (run a 5K, read 24 books, save $5,000). Habits are the daily actions that get you there (run 20 minutes, read 30 pages, transfer $50). Effective goal tracking in Notion combines both: a goals database for the big picture and a habit tracker widget for the daily actions that compound over time.

### Does embedding widgets slow down Notion pages?

Slightly, but it's negligible for most users. Notion loads iframes asynchronously, so they don't block the page from rendering. If you have a very widget-heavy page, consolidate widgets on a dedicated dashboard page and link to it from your goals database rather than embedding on every row.

## What Are the Two Main Ways to Track Mood in Notion?

You can track your mood in Notion either by building a native database with a select or emoji property for daily entries, or by embedding a purpose-built habit tracker widget. The database approach gives you full control over your data structure but requires manual setup and lacks visual feedback like streaks. The widget approach is ready in seconds and shows you patterns at a glance.

### Option 1: Native Notion Database

This method uses Notion's built-in database features. Here's how to set it up:

1. Create a new database (inline or full-page) in Notion
2. Add a **Date** property and set it to today by default
3. Add a **Select** property named "Mood" with options like: Great, Good, Neutral, Low, Rough
4. Optionally add a **Text** property for notes
5. Switch to a **Gallery** or **Calendar** view to visualize entries over time
6. Each day, open the database and add a new row with today's mood

**The downside:** You have to remember to log it every day, there's no reminder or streak counter, and reviewing weekly patterns means manually filtering your database. It works, but it adds friction.

## How Do You Make Daily Mood Tracking Actually Stick?

The biggest reason mood tracking fails is friction. The more steps between you and logging your mood, the less likely you are to do it. Here's what works:

- **Put the tracker on your daily dashboard.** If your mood widget is buried two pages deep in Notion, you'll forget it exists. Add it to the first page you open each day.
- **Track at the same time daily.** Morning review and end-of-day reflection both work. Consistency matters more than timing.
- **Pair mood with other habits.** Tracking mood alongside habits like exercise, sleep, or water intake lets you spot patterns. The [Blocs Habit Tracker](https://blocs.me/blog/how-to-add-habit-tracker-to-notion) is designed for multi-habit daily logging.
- **Keep it binary or low-resolution.** A simple good/bad check-in is easier to maintain than a 10-point scale. You can always add nuance in a notes field later.
- **Review weekly, not daily.** Daily logs are inputs. Weekly reviews are where you get insight. The [Blocs analytics view](https://blocs.me/blog/daily-mood-tracker-for-notion) shows weekly and monthly breakdowns in Pro.

## Is a Mood Tracker the Same as a Habit Tracker?

Not exactly, but they overlap significantly. A habit tracker records whether you did or didn't do something (binary). A mood tracker records how you felt (qualitative or on a scale). In practice, many people track mood as one habit among others: "Did I feel good today? Yes/No" is a valid habit entry. Blocs' habit tracker supports this out of the box, making it a practical [mood tracker for Notion](https://blocs.me/blog/mood-tracker-for-notion) without needing a separate tool.

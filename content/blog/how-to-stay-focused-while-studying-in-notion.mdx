## Why Notion Alone Isn't Enough for Deep Focus

Notion is exceptional for organizing notes, syllabi, and reading lists. But by default it has no built-in time management or focus mechanics. Most students end up with their notes open in Notion and a separate browser tab or phone app running a timer, another app tracking habits, and a sticky note reminding them of deadlines.

Every switch between tools is a small interruption. Research on [task-switching from the APA](https://www.apa.org/topics/research/multitasking) shows that even brief mental blocks from switching tasks can cost as much as 40% of productive time. The fix isn't a new productivity system. It's putting the tools where your work already lives.

## How to Use a Pomodoro Timer Inside Notion

The [Pomodoro technique](https://blocs.me/blog/pomodoro-timer-for-notion) divides study time into 25-minute work intervals followed by a 5-minute break. After four rounds, you take a longer break. It's one of the most evidence-backed focus strategies for students.

With Blocs, you can embed a Pomodoro timer directly into any Notion page using a simple URL block:

### Step 1: Open your Notion study page

Go to the Notion page where you do most of your studying, whether that's a subject dashboard, a daily notes page, or a dedicated study hub.

### Step 2: Add an Embed block

Type `/embed` in Notion and select the Embed option from the menu.

### Step 3: Paste the Blocs Pomodoro URL

Enter this URL: `https://blocs.me/pomodoro`. Notion will render the widget inline. You'll have a live, working Pomodoro timer without leaving your page.

### Step 4: Resize to fit your layout

Drag the widget to a comfortable size. It works well alongside a notes column in a two-column Notion layout.

## Why Notion Users Struggle to Stay Hydrated

If you live in Notion — using it for tasks, notes, journaling, and planning — hydration tends to fall off the radar entirely. You might have a "Health" page buried somewhere, or a database with a water column you stopped filling in after week one. The friction is the problem: opening a separate page, finding the right row, updating a number. It adds up fast.

The fix isn't a better template. It's having a live, interactive widget sitting *on the same page you already use every day*. When your water tracker is next to your task list, you actually see it. When you see it, you drink more water.

## How to Add a Water Tracker to Notion

Blocs widgets embed in Notion using the native "Embed" block. Here's exactly how to do it:

### Step 1: Copy the widget URL

The Blocs Water Tracker URL is: `https://blocs.me/water-tracker`

### Step 2: Open your Notion page

Navigate to the Notion page where you want the tracker to appear — your daily dashboard, a health page, or a morning routine template.

### Step 3: Add an Embed block

Type `/embed` in Notion and select the "Embed" option. Paste the Blocs water tracker URL and press Enter.

### Step 4: Resize to fit

Grab the bottom edge of the embed block and resize it to a comfortable height. For the water tracker, a height of around 400–500px works well. You can also drag it into a two-column layout alongside your task list.

### Step 5: Log your first glass

Click the widget to add your first glass of water. The progress bar updates instantly. That's it — you're set up.

## Why a Visual Tracker Works Better Than a Notion Database

The standard Notion approach to water tracking is a database with a number property. You open it, find today's row, increment the number. If you're disciplined, it works. For most people, it doesn't stick.

A visual widget solves three problems databases can't:

- **Visibility:** A progress bar on your dashboard is impossible to ignore. A database row is easy to forget.
- **Speed:** One click to log a glass. No navigation, no typing.
- **Motivation:** Watching a progress bar fill up is genuinely satisfying. Typing "6" into a cell is not.

This is the same reason [habit tracking widgets outperform Notion databases](https://blocs.me/blog/notion-widgets-for-habit-tracking) for most users — the friction of manual database entry quietly kills habits over time.

## Tips for Actually Drinking More Water

The tracker is only one piece. Here are a few habits that pair well with it:

- **Set a morning intention:** Log one glass first thing in the morning, before checking tasks. It starts the habit early and gets you to 12% of your goal before 9am.
- **Keep the tracker on your main dashboard:** If you have to navigate to a separate page, you'll check it less. Put it on the page you open every day.
- **Pair it with another habit:** Log a glass every time you start a Pomodoro session or finish a task block. Habit stacking is one of the most consistent ways to build new routines.
- **Use streaks as motivation:** With Blocs Pro, streak tracking shows you how many consecutive days you've hit your goal. Breaking a streak is a surprisingly strong motivator.

If you're also tracking other health habits in Notion, the [Blocs Habit Tracker widget](https://blocs.me/habit-tracker-widget) pairs naturally with the water tracker and can sit in the same dashboard layout.

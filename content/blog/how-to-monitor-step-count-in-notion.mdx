## Why Does Notion Fall Short for Step Tracking?

Notion is a flexible workspace, but it has no built-in numeric goal widget or progress visualization for health metrics. Most people resort to a simple number property in a database, which requires creating a new row every day, building formulas for percentage progress, and adding conditional formatting just to see a bar. That setup works, but it is fragile and tedious to maintain. A dedicated embed is faster and more reliable.

## How Do You Embed a Step Tracker in Notion?

Copy the widget URL and paste it into any Notion page using the **/embed** block. Notion wraps it in an iframe automatically. Here is the step-by-step process:

1. Open (or create) the Notion page where you want to monitor your steps.
2. Type `/embed` and press Enter to insert an embed block.
3. Paste the widget URL: `https://blocs.me/progress-bar` or `https://blocs.me/habit-tracker`.
4. Click **Embed link**. The widget appears inline on your page.
5. Resize the block by dragging the corners to fit your layout.
6. Set your step goal inside the widget and start logging.

The embed persists across devices, so your step tracker is visible whether you open Notion on desktop, mobile, or browser. This is a core advantage over manual database tables, which often look broken on mobile.

## Should You Log Steps Manually or Automate the Sync?

Manual logging suits most Notion users. You check your phone's health app (Apple Health, Google Fit, Fitbit) once a day, note your count, and update the widget. This takes under a minute and keeps Notion as your central dashboard without complex integrations.

For automated syncing, tools like [Zapier](https://zapier.com) or [Make (formerly Integromat)](https://make.com) can push step data from Apple Health or Google Fit into a Notion database property, but this requires setting up a workflow and is overkill for most personal use cases. The Blocs widget approach is intentionally low-friction — it is designed for daily check-ins, not background data pipelines.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does Notion have a built-in step counter?

Notion does not have a native step counter or fitness tracking feature. You can build a manual logging database with number properties, but there is no built-in progress visualization. Embedding a third-party widget like Blocs is the fastest way to get a visual step tracker inside Notion.

### Can Notion sync with Apple Health or Google Fit for step data?

Notion does not natively sync with Apple Health or Google Fit. You can connect them via automation tools like [Zapier](https://zapier.com) or [Make](https://make.com), but this requires configuration and a paid plan on those platforms. Most users find manual daily entry into a Notion widget faster and simpler.

### Is there a free step tracker widget for Notion?

Yes. Blocs offers a free Habit Tracker embed that you can use to log daily step goals with no sign-up required. The Progress Bar widget is a Pro feature, but the Habit Tracker free tier covers the core use case of tracking whether you hit your target each day.

### Will the step tracker widget work on Notion mobile?

Yes. Blocs widgets are responsive iframes that render correctly in Notion's mobile app on both iOS and Android. Your data syncs to the cloud (with Pro) so progress you log on mobile is reflected when you open Notion on desktop.

### Can I track multiple health goals on one Notion page?

Yes. You can embed multiple Blocs widgets on a single Notion page — for example, a Habit Tracker with step, water, and sleep habits all in one widget. With Blocs Pro, each habit has its own goal, streak counter, and analytics view.

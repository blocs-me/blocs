## What is a habit tracker calendar for Notion?

A habit tracker calendar for Notion is a widget or embed that displays your daily habit completions as a visual grid, similar to a GitHub contribution graph, directly inside a Notion page. Instead of scrolling through database rows, you see a month at a glance with colored cells marking which days you kept a streak alive.

Most Notion users try to build this with a database, a rollup property, and a gallery view styled to look like a calendar. It works, but it takes real setup time and breaks easily when you duplicate the template. Blocs replaces that entire process with a single embed block.

## Why use a calendar view instead of a checklist?

A calendar view surfaces streaks and gaps at a glance, which a checklist or table buries in rows. Seeing an unbroken run of colored squares is a stronger behavioral cue to keep going than scanning a list of checkboxes, which is part of why streak-based tracking apps have become the default pattern for habit tools outside Notion too ([James Clear, Atomic Habits habit tracker guide](https://jamesclear.com/habit-tracker)).

It also solves a layout problem specific to Notion: building a true calendar heatmap with native databases means manually styling rollups per date, which most templates skip in favor of a simple checkbox table. An embedded calendar widget like Blocs' gets you the heatmap view without the formula work.

## Is a habit tracker calendar better than a Notion database?

For visual streak tracking, yes: an embedded calendar widget shows your consistency at a glance, while a native Notion database requires manual setup to approximate the same view. Databases are better if you need to cross-reference habits with other Notion data (projects, tasks); a widget is better for a clean, dedicated tracking view.

Many people combine both: a Notion database for planning and a Blocs calendar widget embedded on the same page for the at-a-glance view. You're not locked into one approach.

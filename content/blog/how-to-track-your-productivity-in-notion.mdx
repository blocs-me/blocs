## Why doesn't Notion track productivity natively?

Notion is a workspace builder, not a productivity app, so it has no native timer, streak counter, or focus tracker. Everything you see (databases, formulas, rollups) is a building block you assemble yourself, which is powerful but time-consuming for something as simple as "did I work today?"

Research on attention and task-switching, including work summarized by the [American Psychological Association](https://www.apa.org/topics/research/multitasking), consistently shows that fragmented work sessions reduce output quality. That's exactly the gap tools like timers and habit trackers are built to close: they impose structure Notion itself doesn't provide.

## FAQs

### Does Notion have a built-in productivity tracker?

No. Notion has databases and formulas you can configure into a tracker, but there's no native timer, streak counter, or focus-session feature. Most people either build a manual setup or embed a widget like Blocs to get that functionality faster.

### What's the difference between time tracking and productivity tracking?

Time tracking logs how long you spend on tasks; productivity tracking measures output and consistency, like completed focus sessions or daily streaks. A Pomodoro timer addresses the former, while a habit tracker addresses the latter. For a deeper comparison, see [best time tracking Notion widgets](https://blocs.me/blog/best-time-tracking-notion-widgets).

### Will Blocs widgets slow down my Notion page?

No noticeable impact for typical use. Each widget loads as a lightweight iframe, similar to embedding a YouTube video or Google Calendar in Notion.

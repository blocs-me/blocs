## What Is the Pomodoro Technique?

The Pomodoro Technique is a time management method developed by Francesco Cirillo in the late 1980s. The core idea: work in focused 25-minute blocks (called "pomodoros"), then take a 5-minute break. After four pomodoros, take a longer 15-30 minute break.

It's one of the most studied productivity methods around. [Research cited by Todoist](https://todoist.com/productivity-methods/pomodoro-technique) shows that working in short, time-boxed intervals reduces the impact of interruptions and helps you maintain focus over longer periods. The technique also makes large tasks feel less overwhelming by breaking them into smaller, timed chunks.

Standard Pomodoro intervals:

- **Work session:** 25 minutes
- **Short break:** 5 minutes
- **Long break:** 15-30 minutes (after every 4 sessions)

## Tips for Actually Sticking With the Pomodoro Technique

The Pomodoro Technique is simple in theory but takes a few sessions to build into a habit. A few things that help:

- **Start with one pomodoro.** Don't plan a full 4-session block to begin with. Just commit to one 25-minute session and see how it feels.
- **Define your task before you start the timer.** Vague tasks lead to unfocused sessions. Write down the specific output you want before clicking start.
- **Actually take the breaks.** Skipping breaks defeats the purpose. The short break is where your brain consolidates what it just processed.
- **Close other tabs during the session.** The timer being in Notion helps, but you still need to reduce other sources of interruption.
- **Don't restart a session you've already started.** If you get interrupted, note it and keep going. Interruptions are data, not failures.

According to [research published by the American Psychological Association](https://www.apa.org/monitor/2016/06/multitasking), task-switching can reduce productivity by as much as 40%. The Pomodoro Technique is one of the most practical ways to counter this by committing to a single task for a bounded period of time.

## What is a reading goal tracker for Notion?

A reading goal tracker for Notion is a tool — either a database template, an embedded widget, or both — that lets you set a target (books per year, pages per day, or minutes per session) and log progress without leaving Notion. The best implementations update visually in real time so you always know where you stand against your goal.

Notion's built-in databases are flexible, but they don't give you live progress visuals or streak tracking out of the box. You'd need to build formulas, maintain rollup properties, and manually update entries to get close to what a dedicated widget delivers automatically. That setup works for power users, but most readers just want to open their workspace, log a session, and see their streak — not debug formula syntax.

Embedded widgets solve this by handling the logic for you. You get a live, interactive tracker that sits right on your Notion page.

## How do you track reading goals in Notion with Blocs?

Blocs offers two widgets that together cover the full reading goal tracking workflow: the [Habit Tracker](https://blocs.me/habit-tracker-widget) for daily logging and streaks, and the [Progress Bar](https://blocs.me/progress-bar-widget) for visualizing long-term goals like "read 24 books this year." Here's how to set them up.

### Step 1: Embed the Habit Tracker in your Notion page

Open the page where you want your reading tracker to live. Type `/embed` and paste this URL:

```
https://blocs.me/habit-tracker
```

Notion will render the widget as an interactive iframe. Resize it to fit your layout.

### Step 2: Configure your reading habit

Inside the widget, add a habit — something like "Read 20 pages" or "Read 30 minutes." The free tier supports default settings immediately with no account required. Each day you complete your reading session, tap the habit to log it. The widget tracks your current streak and overall completion rate automatically.

### Step 3: Add a Progress Bar for annual or monthly goals (Pro)

If you're tracking a books-per-year goal, embed the [Progress Bar widget](https://blocs.me/progress-bar-widget) on the same page. Set the total to your target (say, 24 books) and increment it each time you finish a book. The bar updates live and gives you an at-a-glance view of where you are in the year.

```
https://blocs.me/progress-bar
```

The Progress Bar is a Pro feature, but at $17 one-time it pays for itself quickly compared to annual subscriptions charged by other tools.

## Why not just use a Notion database for reading goals?

A Notion database gives you a manual reading log. Blocs gives you a live, interactive tracker. Both have their place, but they serve different purposes. A database is great for storing book metadata — title, author, genre, rating, notes. A widget is better for the daily habit loop: did you read today? What's your streak? How close are you to your annual target?

The most effective Notion reading setups combine both: a linked database for your book list and embedded Blocs widgets for the habit and goal tracking layer. You get the organizational depth of a database with the motivational feedback of a live tracker — all on one page.

## What are the best reading goal setups for Notion?

### Yearly reading challenge

Embed a Progress Bar set to your book target (12, 24, 52 — whatever your goal is). Each time you finish a book, increment the counter. Pair it with a Countdown Timer showing days left in the year so you always know your pace. Both widgets sit on your Notion dashboard and update in real time.

### Daily reading habit

Use the Habit Tracker with a single habit: "Read." Log it each day when you hit your page or time target. The streak counter is surprisingly effective for maintaining consistency — the same mechanism that makes apps like Duolingo sticky, applied directly to your Notion workspace. Research on habit formation consistently shows that [visual streak tracking increases follow-through](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3505409/) on daily goals.

### Book club or reading sprint

Set a Countdown Timer to your next book club meeting and a Progress Bar tracking chapters completed. This works well for group reads where everyone needs to finish by a specific date. The deadline pressure of a live countdown is more motivating than a static date in a calendar entry.

### Student or research reading

Track academic reading separately from leisure reading by embedding two Habit Tracker widgets on different pages — one for your research queue, one for personal books. Blocs Pro supports unlimited habits and cloud sync, so your progress follows you across devices.

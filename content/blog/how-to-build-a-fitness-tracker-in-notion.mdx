## What is a Notion fitness tracker?

A Notion fitness tracker is a database or page inside Notion used to log workouts, body metrics, water intake, and health habits in one place. Most people build it from a table database with properties like exercise name, sets, reps, weight, and date, then add views to filter by week or workout type.

Notion's own [template gallery](https://www.notion.so/templates) offers free starting points for fitness and workout tracking, which you can duplicate and customize rather than building from scratch.

## How do you build a workout log database in Notion?

Create a new database (table view), then add properties for Date, Exercise, Sets, Reps, Weight, and a relation to a "Workout Days" page if you want to group exercises by session. Add a formula property for total volume (sets × reps × weight) if you want progress math done automatically.

### Step-by-step setup

- **1. Create the database.** Type `/table` in a Notion page and choose "Table - Full page."
- **2. Add core properties.** Date, Exercise Name, Category (e.g., Legs, Cardio, Upper Body), Sets, Reps, Weight, Notes.
- **3. Add views.** Create a Board view grouped by Category, and a Calendar view by Date so you can see training frequency at a glance.
- **4. Add a formula (optional).** Use `prop("Sets") * prop("Reps") * prop("Weight")` to auto-calculate training volume per entry.
- **5. Build a dashboard page.** Link your database views plus any widgets (see below) on a single "Fitness" page for a one-stop view.

## How do you embed a widget in Notion?

To embed a Blocs widget, go to [blocs.me/pricing](https://blocs.me/pricing) or the widget's dedicated page, copy the embed URL, then in Notion type `/embed` and paste the link. Notion renders it as a live iframe that updates and syncs in real time, no plugin or sign-up required for the free widgets.

### Setup steps

- Open the widget page (e.g., [blocs.me/habit-tracker-widget](https://blocs.me/habit-tracker-widget))
- Copy the widget's embed URL
- In your Notion fitness page, type `/embed` and select "Embed link"
- Paste the URL and resize the block to fit your layout

## What should you track for general fitness goals?

For general fitness, track workout frequency (habit streak), daily water intake, body weight trend, and session notes - that's enough data to see patterns without turning tracking into a chore. The [CDC's physical activity guidelines](https://www.cdc.gov/physicalactivity/basics/adults/index.htm) recommend at least 150 minutes of moderate activity per week, which a simple habit tracker can log day by day.

## FAQs

### Do I need a Notion template to build a fitness tracker?

No. Notion's [template gallery](https://www.notion.so/templates) offers starting points, but you can build a fully functional fitness tracker from scratch using a single table database with 15 minutes of setup.

### Can I track workouts and habits without any paid tools?

Yes. Notion's database features are free, and Blocs' basic Water Tracker and Habit Tracker widgets are free with no sign-up, which covers hydration and streak tracking at no cost.

### What's the difference between a habit tracker and a workout database?

A habit tracker shows yes/no completion and streaks for recurring goals (like "worked out today"), while a workout database stores detailed exercise data like sets, reps, and weight for progress analysis over time.

### Can I see weekly or monthly fitness trends in Notion?

Database views (Calendar, Board, Timeline) show trends from your logged data, and Blocs Pro adds built-in weekly/monthly analytics directly inside the habit and water tracker widgets without manual charting.

### Will embedding widgets slow down my Notion page?

No. Blocs widgets are lightweight iframes designed for Notion embeds and load independently of your page content, so they don't affect database performance.

### Does Blocs offer a dedicated workout or exercise widget?

Not yet. Blocs currently covers water, habits, timers, and goal progress, and a dedicated workout-logging widget is on the roadmap.

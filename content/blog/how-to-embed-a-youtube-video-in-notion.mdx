## What is the easiest way to embed a YouTube video in Notion?

The easiest way is to copy the video's URL from YouTube, paste it on an empty line in your Notion page, and choose "Embed" from the menu that appears. Notion automatically detects YouTube links and renders a playable video block, no extra steps or third-party tools required.

### Method 1: Paste the link directly

- Open the YouTube video and copy its URL (the share link or the one in your address bar).
- Click into any Notion page and paste it on its own line.
- A small menu appears with options like "Create bookmark," "Create embed," and "Paste as link." Choose **Embed**.
- Resize the block by dragging its corner once it loads.

### Method 2: Use the /video slash command

- Type `/video` and press enter on the "Video" block option.
- Paste the YouTube URL into the field that appears and click "Embed link."
- This method is more reliable for links that don't auto-trigger the embed menu, and it's also how you'd embed self-hosted or non-YouTube video files.

### Method 3: Generic /embed block

If the video-specific block doesn't render correctly, try `/embed` instead. It uses Notion's broader embed system (the same one used for Figma, Google Maps, and other iframe-based content) and tends to catch edge cases the dedicated video block misses. This is the same underlying mechanism covered in our guide on [how to embed a website in Notion](https://blocs.me/blog/how-to-embed-a-website-in-notion).

## Why won't my YouTube video embed in Notion?

A video usually fails to embed in Notion because it's set to private, age-restricted, or has embedding disabled by the uploader in YouTube's own settings. Notion can only display what YouTube's player allows; if the creator blocked external embedding, Notion will show a blank or broken block regardless of the method used.

Before troubleshooting further, confirm the video plays correctly on a fresh, logged-out YouTube tab. If it doesn't play there, it won't play in Notion either. YouTube's own [video embedding help page](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/171780) explains how creators can toggle embedding permissions from Studio, which is the most common root cause.

## Can you embed a YouTube playlist in Notion?

Yes, you can embed a full YouTube playlist in Notion the same way you'd embed a single video: paste the playlist URL and select "Embed," or use `/video` and paste the playlist link into the field. Notion renders the playlist as a scrollable player rather than a single clip.

## FAQs

### Does embedding a YouTube video in Notion cost anything?

No. Embedding YouTube videos in Notion is a native, free feature available on every Notion plan, including the free tier. There's no premium requirement or add-on needed to paste and embed a video link.

### Will the embedded video autoplay in Notion?

No, embedded YouTube videos in Notion do not autoplay by default. Visitors need to click the play button manually, which matches standard YouTube embed behavior used across most websites.

### Can I embed a YouTube Short in Notion?

Yes, YouTube Shorts can be embedded the same way as regular videos: copy the Shorts URL and paste it into Notion, then select Embed. The aspect ratio will display as a vertical frame rather than the standard widescreen player.

### Does embedding a video slow down my Notion page?

Slightly, since each embed loads an iframe that pulls data from YouTube's servers. Pages with many video embeds can feel slower to load than text-only pages, so it's worth limiting embeds to one or two per page for performance.

### Can I embed a video on a Notion page I share publicly?

Yes, embedded videos display for anyone viewing a published or shared Notion page, as long as the underlying YouTube video itself is public or unlisted. Private videos won't play even if the Notion page is shared.

### What's the difference between /video and /embed in Notion?

The `/video` block is purpose-built for video URLs (YouTube, Vimeo, direct video files), while `/embed` is a general-purpose block for any iframe-compatible content, including maps, PDFs, and widgets. Both can render a YouTube link, but `/video` is the more direct option.

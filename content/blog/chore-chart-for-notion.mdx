## What is a chore chart for Notion?

A chore chart for Notion is a visual, repeating checklist embedded in a workspace page that tracks who did which household task and when. Instead of a printed fridge chart, it lives inside the Notion page your family already checks daily, using a widget or database table.

Most people start by building a Notion database with columns for task, assignee, and date, then manually mark rows complete. That works, but it means creating recurring templates, writing rollup formulas for streaks, and rebuilding the view every week. A widget like Blocs' [Habit Tracker](https://blocs.me/habit-tracker-widget) skips all of that - it's purpose-built for "did this happen today/this week" tracking, which is exactly the chore-chart use case.

## How do you make a chore chart in Notion?

To make a chore chart in Notion, copy a widget embed URL (such as [Blocs' Habit Tracker](https://blocs.me/habit-tracker-widget)), paste it into an `/embed` block on any Notion page, and resize the block. No sign-up is required for the free version, and the chart updates live inside your workspace.

### Step 1: Get your embed link

Visit the [Habit Tracker widget page](https://blocs.me/habit-tracker-widget) and copy the direct embed URL. This is the same approach covered in Blocs' [guide to adding a habit tracker to Notion](https://blocs.me/blog/how-to-add-habit-tracker-to-notion).

### Step 2: Add an embed block in Notion

Type `/embed` in any Notion page, paste the widget URL, and press enter. Notion renders it as a live iframe.

### Step 3: Rename chores to match your household

Edit the task labels directly in the widget - "Dishes," "Trash," "Vacuum" - and check them off as they're completed.

### Step 4: Resize and position it

Drag the block edges to fit your layout. Most people place it on a shared family dashboard or weekly planner page.

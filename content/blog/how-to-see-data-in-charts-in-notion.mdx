## What Is Notion's Chart View?

Notion's Chart view is a database visualization mode that converts rows and properties into bar charts, line charts, or donut charts. It was introduced as part of Notion's push toward [richer database views](https://www.notion.com/help/database-views) and is available to users on the Plus plan ($10/month) and above. You can group by any property, filter by date, and display counts or numeric roll-ups visually.

### How to Enable Chart View in Notion

1. Open any database (table, board, or list view).
2. Click **+ Add a view** in the top-left tab bar.
3. Select **Chart** from the view type list.
4. Choose your chart type: bar, line, or donut.
5. Set the **X-axis** (a property like Date or Status) and the **Y-axis** (a count or numeric property).
6. Add filters if you want to scope to a specific date range or tag.

Once configured, the chart updates in real time as you add or edit database rows. This works well for project tracking, task completion rates, content calendars, and CRM-style pipelines where all your data already lives in a Notion database.

## What Does Notion Chart View Not Cover?

Notion charts only visualize data that already exists as rows in a Notion database. They can't chart your daily water intake, your Pomodoro session history, your habit streaks, or your focus time unless you manually log every entry as a database row, which is tedious and breaks the flow of work. That's the gap that embeddable widgets fill.

Common use cases Notion's chart view doesn't handle well:

- Daily hydration tracking with visual cup-by-cup progress
- Habit streak charts (daily check-ins with weekly/monthly views)
- Pomodoro session counts and focus-time analytics over the week
- Goal progress bars that update automatically

## FAQs

### Is Notion's Chart view available on the free plan?

No. As of 2026, Notion's Chart view requires a Plus plan ($10/month) or higher. Free Notion users cannot add a Chart view to their databases. Embedded widgets like Blocs are a free alternative for visualizing tracking data inside Notion pages.

### What chart types does Notion support natively?

Notion supports bar charts, line charts, and donut charts in its Chart view. You can group by any database property and display row counts or numeric aggregations. It does not support scatter plots, gantt-style charts, or custom color schemes beyond basic options.

### Can I see a chart of my habit completions in Notion?

Not with Notion's native features without manually logging each check-in as a database row. The [Blocs Habit Tracker](https://blocs.me/blog/habit-tracker-for-notion) handles this automatically: check off habits in the widget, and Pro gives you weekly/monthly completion charts without any manual data entry.

### Can I embed an external chart in Notion?

Yes. Notion's `/Embed` block accepts any URL that supports iframe embedding. This includes Blocs widgets, Google Data Studio reports, Loom videos, and many other tools. For interactive tracking charts, embedding a Blocs widget is one of the fastest setups available.

### Do I need a Notion database to create a chart?

Only for Notion's native Chart view, which requires a database as its data source. Embedded widgets do not require a Notion database at all. They store and visualize their own data independently, which is why they work on any Notion page including the free plan.

### Is Blocs Pro worth it for analytics?

If you're actively tracking habits, hydration, or focus time inside Notion, the weekly/monthly analytics and streaks in Blocs Pro add real visibility. At a one-time $17 payment with no recurring subscription, the cost is lower than two months of a typical productivity app subscription.

## How to Set Up Your Daily Journal Database in Notion

### Step 1: Create a new database

Open Notion, create a new page, and type `/database`. Choose "Database - Full page". Name it something like "Daily Journal". Add these properties:

- **Date** (Date type) - auto-filled with today's date via a template.
- **Mood** (Select type) - options like Great / Good / Okay / Rough.
- **Gratitude** (Text type) - one or two lines.
- **Focus Word** (Text type) - one word to anchor the day.
- **Wins** (Text type) - what went well.
- **Tomorrow** (Text type) - one thing to prepare for.

### Step 2: Build a daily entry template

Inside your database, click the dropdown arrow next to the blue "New" button and select "New template". Build a page with all the sections you want pre-filled as headers. Include a body section for freeform writing. When you're done, click back to the database view and use this template for every new entry.

### Step 3: Add a template button to your dashboard

On your main Notion dashboard, type `/button` to create a button block. Configure it to "Add page to" your journal database using the template you just created. One click each morning gives you a fresh entry with today's date pre-set.

### Step 4: Create a filtered "Today" view

In your journal database, add a new view (Gallery or List works well). Filter by Date = Today. This becomes your default view so yesterday's entries don't clutter your screen.

## How to Embed Widgets Into Your Notion Journal

Notion supports iframes via the `/embed` block. Paste a widget URL into the embed block and it renders interactively inside your page. This means you can add a live habit tracker, a running Pomodoro timer, or a water intake counter directly alongside your writing, with no app switching.

Here's the embed workflow:

1. Open your Notion journal page (or your daily dashboard).
2. Type `/embed` and press Enter.
3. Paste the widget URL (e.g., `https://blocs.me/habit-tracker`).
4. Click "Embed link". Resize by dragging the bottom edge of the block.

## Tips to Make Your Notion Journal Actually Stick

- **Keep it short by design.** A journal with 5 pre-filled prompts is easier to complete than a blank page. Use your template to guide, not constrain.
- **Journal on the same page as your task list.** If you already open that page every day, you'll see your journal entry waiting for you.
- **Use the habit tracker as your anchor.** The streak is the product. Once you've got 7 days in a row, you won't want to break it.
- **Don't aim for length.** Three honest sentences beat three empty paragraphs. Short entries compound over time.
- **Review weekly.** A quick 5-minute read of last week's entries builds self-awareness faster than the journaling itself.

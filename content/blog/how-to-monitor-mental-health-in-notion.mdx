## What Should You Actually Track for Mental Health?

Focus on observable daily markers rather than trying to quantify subjective states with precision. The goal is to notice trends over days and weeks, not to score yourself. The most useful markers are: mood (a 1-5 scale or simple emoji), sleep hours, water intake, physical movement, and one or two personal habit goals like journaling or meditation. Tracking all five gives you enough data to correlate bad-mood days with poor sleep or low hydration without overwhelming your check-in routine.

Research consistently shows that [self-monitoring behaviours](https://www.apa.org/monitor/2012/11/gratitude) — writing down moods, habits, and physical patterns — increases awareness and supports behaviour change. Notion is well-suited for this because it's already where many people plan their work and personal projects, so adding a wellness layer costs almost no extra context-switching.

## How Do You Stay Consistent With Mental Health Tracking?

The biggest predictor of whether a Notion wellness dashboard works is whether you open it every day. Keep your check-in under five minutes total — if it takes longer, you'll skip it. Pin the dashboard to your sidebar, set a recurring Notion reminder, and anchor the check-in to an existing routine (morning coffee, end-of-workday). Start with fewer metrics and add more only after the daily habit sticks.

- **Use the widgets, not just the database.** Tapping a water cup is faster than typing a number into a database property. Interactive embeds reduce friction significantly.
- **Review weekly, not just daily.** Set a Sunday reminder to open your mood database and filter by the past seven days. Patterns are invisible day-to-day.
- **Don't aim for perfect data.** A week of patchy logs is still useful. Missing a day isn't a failure — skip the guilt and log the next day.
- **Pair tracking with journaling.** Numbers tell you what happened; journal entries tell you why. Even two sentences per day adds meaningful context.

## Is Notion Better Than a Dedicated Mental Health App?

Notion is not a replacement for clinical tools or therapy, but for everyday self-monitoring it has a key advantage: you're already there. Dedicated wellness apps require you to remember to open a second app, maintain a second login, and operate in a context separate from your actual work and planning. Embedding wellness tracking directly into your Notion workspace means your mood log and habit tracker live next to your projects, calendar, and goals — which makes the data more contextually meaningful and the habit easier to maintain.

The downside is that Notion has no native notification system for habit reminders, and its database filtering takes some setup to get useful views.

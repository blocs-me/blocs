## What Are Interactive Notion Widgets?

Notion is a powerful workspace, but it doesn't natively support interactive elements like live timers, habit checkboxes with streaks, or water intake counters. Interactive widgets fill that gap -- they're small web apps that you embed directly into a Notion page using Notion's built-in embed block.

Once embedded, they behave like mini-apps inside your workspace. You can start a Pomodoro session, log a glass of water, or check off a habit without ever leaving Notion. The widget lives alongside your notes, tasks, and databases -- no tab switching needed.

## How to Add a Widget to Notion: Step-by-Step

Adding a widget takes less than a minute. Here's the process:

1. Open any Notion page where you want the widget to appear.
2. Type **/embed** and press Enter to insert an embed block.
3. Paste the widget URL into the embed field. For example, to add the Blocs Pomodoro timer, paste `https://blocs.me/pomodoro`.
4. Click **Embed link**. The widget will load inline on your page.
5. Resize the embed block by dragging the handles to fit your layout.

That's it. The widget is now interactive -- you can click, input data, and track progress directly from your Notion page. Your settings and data persist even after you close the page.

## Why Embed Widgets Instead of Building Databases?

Notion databases are great for structured data, but they have real limitations for real-time tracking:

- **No built-in timers.** A Notion database can't count down or run a Pomodoro session. You need to manually log entries after the fact.
- **No live progress visualization.** Progress bars in Notion require formulas and workarounds. A dedicated widget shows a clean visual with no setup.
- **No ambient data.** Databases don't display live weather, current time, or a daily quote without complex third-party automation.
- **Friction adds up.** Every manual log entry is a small distraction. An embedded widget reduces that to a single tap.

Widgets don't replace Notion databases -- they complement them. Use a database to plan and document; use widgets to track and stay focused in the moment.

## Tips for Embedding Widgets Effectively

### Use columns to save vertical space

Notion supports multi-column layouts. Drag your embed block next to a text block or database to keep your page compact. A Pomodoro timer and a habit tracker can sit side by side in a two-column layout.

### Create a dedicated dashboard page

Rather than scattering widgets across multiple pages, build a single "Daily Dashboard" page with your most-used widgets embedded at the top. Link to it from your Notion sidebar for one-click access.

### Pin it to a template

If you use Notion's template feature for daily notes or weekly reviews, add your widget embeds to the template. Every new page will include the widget automatically.

### Resize for your workflow

Tall widgets like the water tracker look best at around 350-400px wide. Compact widgets like the progress bar can sit at full column width. Experiment with the drag handles to find what feels right.

Free widgets store data locally in your browser. Pro users get cloud sync, so data persists across devices and browsers. If you use the free tier on multiple devices, progress won't carry over.

### Can I embed multiple widgets on the same Notion page?

Yes. You can add as many embed blocks as you want. Use Notion's column layout to keep the page tidy if you're embedding several widgets at once.

### Does Blocs work on Notion mobile?

Notion mobile apps have limited support for embedded iframes. Widgets work best on Notion's desktop app and web version. Mobile support depends on how Notion handles embeds on iOS and Android.

## What Are the Alternatives to a Notion Step Counter?

If you want to track steps in Notion but aren't sold on the widget approach, here are the main options — and their trade-offs:

### Manual Notion Database

You can create a Notion database with a "Steps" number property and log each day manually. It works, but it's entirely passive — no visual streak, no progress bar, no motivation layer. It's also one more database to maintain in your workspace. As covered in the [Blocs guide on monitoring step count in Notion](https://blocs.me/blog/how-to-monitor-step-count-in-notion), the manual database route works best if you want raw data export, not daily motivation.

### Separate Mobile Fitness Apps

Apps like Apple Health, Google Fit, or Garmin Connect auto-track steps via your phone's accelerometer. They're accurate and automatic — but they're separate apps. If your goal is to keep fitness tracking inside Notion alongside your goals and journaling, these don't help with consolidation.

### Blocs Habit Tracker Widget

Not fully automatic (you still check off your daily step goal manually), but it adds the motivational layer — streaks, visual progress, analytics — that a bare database can't provide. ## Frequently Asked Questions

### Does Notion have a built-in step counter?

Notion does not have a native step counter. You can log steps manually in a Notion database, but there's no built-in visual tracker or streak feature. Third-party embeddable widgets like the Blocs Habit Tracker are the closest equivalent currently available inside Notion.

### Can I sync Apple Health or Google Fit steps to Notion?

Not natively. Notion doesn't have a direct API integration with Apple Health or Google Fit. Some users use automation tools like Zapier or Make (formerly Integromat) to push step data from Health apps into a Notion database, but this requires a paid automation plan and technical setup. The simpler approach is manual daily check-off using a habit tracker widget.
